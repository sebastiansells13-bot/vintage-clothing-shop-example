## Deliberately not used

A few candidates turned up during sourcing that were rejected even though their
licenses would have technically allowed reuse:

- **A close-up product photo of a real, identifiable model** wearing a sweater
  (face clearly visible, direct to camera). A Creative Commons license covers
  copyright reproduction of the photographer's work — it doesn't grant a model
  release for using a real, identifiable person's likeness in what amounts to
  advertising for a fictional shop they never agreed to represent. Same reasoning
  as the realty-agent-example's decision to skip a stock headshot.
- **An extreme close-up of a museum-catalogued jacket** (accession-numbered, with
  what appear to be military service-stripe insignia). Repurposing what looks like
  a historically significant service garment as casual resale merchandise felt
  tonally wrong, independent of licensing.

Where a suitable photo genuinely didn't exist (Jackets, Sweaters), the honest move
was an original graphic rather than stretching a mismatched or inappropriate photo
to fit.
